Before you draw — the 30-second check

  1. Google the subject name. Not “<subject> t shirt” — the name itself. If the best result is a Facebook thread, a forum post, or nothing, that is the signal.
  2. Could a marketplace swap in another city? “Retro Tacoma” yes, “Calico Cat Motel” no. If yes, Etsy and TeePublic already sell it and we lose.
  3. Is the business still trading? Then it’s a licensing conversation, not a free design — and a link from their site is worth more than the margin on the shirt.
  4. Search the name at tmsearch.uspto.gov. A live mark is a veto, not a score.
  5. Do we know something nobody wrote down? A first-hand detail — the tiled floor, the smell, who ran it. That is what actually wins the ranking.

If Keyword Planner shows 1,000+ searches a month, skip it — that’s a national keyword and we lose. Use it to reject things that are too big, never to choose between things that are small.

Why there’s no search-volume column

Because every tool that reports volume would print zero next to every subject on this page, and a zero would read as a verdict. SEMrush builds its database at national level and drops terms below a threshold; Keyword Planner buckets small numbers into ranges wide enough to hide a six-fold difference. “Clyde Rushton Puyallup water slide” returns no row in either of them — and 253gear holds #1 for it.

A zero from a keyword tool means below threshold, never no demand. So the number on each row is vacancy, scored by actually searching the subject and reading what comes back. It is slower, and it is the only one of the two that predicted the win we already have.
